GEOTIFF SATELLITE ANALYSIS ENGINE: DETAILED & SIMPLE BREAKDOWN
================================================================================

WHAT IS A GEOTIFF?
  "Think of a standard JPEG or PNG photo—it only stores Red, Green, and Blue 
  colors. A GeoTIFF is a high-tech satellite image. Every single pixel in a 
  GeoTIFF knows its exact GPS location on Earth and contains invisible light data 
  (like Near-Infrared light) captured by satellites in space."

HOW JAL-DRISHTI ANALYZES GEOTIFFS (STEP-BY-STEP):
Our system processes raw satellite files directly inside the web browser using 
JavaScript (geotiff.js) without needing heavy server software like QGIS or Python.

Step 1: Extracting Light Bands
  Satellite cameras capture different wavelengths (bands) of light reflected 
  from Earth:
    - Band 4 (Red Light): Plants absorb red light for photosynthesis.
    - Band 8 (Near-Infrared / NIR): Healthy leaf cells strongly reflect NIR light.
    - Band 3 (Green Light): Water bodies reflect green light.
  Our engine opens the uploaded .tif file and extracts these raw light values 
  into high-speed numerical arrays (Float32Array).

Step 2: Running Spectral Formulas (NDVI & NDWI)
  For every single pixel in the satellite scene, the engine calculates two 
  scientific indices:
    1. NDVI (Vegetation Density Index):
       NDVI = (Near Infrared - Red) / (Near Infrared + Red)
       * Acts like a health checkup for plants.
       * High Score (+0.4 to +0.8) = Dense green crops or healthy forest.
       * Low Score (0.0 to +0.2) = Bare soil or dry earth.

    2. NDWI (Surface Water Index):
       NDWI = (Green - Near Infrared) / (Green + Near Infrared)
       * Acts like a water detector.
       * Positive Score (>0) = Open surface water (ponds, check dams, lakes).

Step 3: Precise Area Calculation (Hectares & km²)
  - Sentinel-2 satellite data has a 10m x 10m spatial resolution.
  - This means 1 pixel = 100 square meters (0.01 hectares).
  - The engine counts all pixels matching vegetation or water, multiplies by 
    100 m², and outputs the exact area in Hectares (ha) and Square Kilometers (km²).
